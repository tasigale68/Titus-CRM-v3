THE 18 NDIS QMS COMPLIANCE REGISTERS:

Under the NDIS Practice Standards, registered providers must maintain quality management records across multiple areas. These 18 registers cover every requirement that NDIS auditors assess during certification and verification audits.

Governance and Risk registers track organisational risks, legislative obligations, continuous improvement activities, and internal audit findings. Incidents and Safety registers document all incidents including those reportable to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission within 5 business days, corrective actions, and any use of restrictive practices. Complaints and Feedback registers capture both complaints and compliments from participants, families, and staff. Workforce and Screening registers track worker screening clearances (NDIS Worker Screening Check, Working with Children Check, police checks), staff training completion, qualifications, and key personnel changes. Participant Records registers maintain individual participant files, signed service agreements, consent records, and medication administration logs.

Six of these 18 registers can be automatically populated from operational data if your provider management software supports it: incident reports, reportable incidents, worker screening checks, staff training records, key personnel records, and service agreements.

WHAT IS THE SCHADS AWARD?

The Social, Community, Home Care and Disability Services Industry Award 2010 (SCHADS Award) is the Fair Work instrument that governs pay rates, penalty rates, and working conditions for employees in the disability, community, and home care sectors in Australia. The SCHADS Award applies to most NDIS support workers, team leaders, and coordinators employed under an employment contract (not independent contractors).

SCHADS PENALTY RATE STRUCTURE:

Ordinary hours (Monday to Friday): 100% of base rate
Saturday: 150% of base rate (x1.5 loading)
Sunday: 200% of base rate (x2.0 loading)
Public holidays: 250% of base rate (x2.5 loading)
Overtime (first 2 hours): 150% of base rate
Overtime (after 2 hours): 200% of base rate
Sleepover allowance: Flat allowance per sleepover plus penalty rates if called to work during the sleepover period
Broken shift allowance: Additional payment when a shift includes an unpaid break of more than one hour

For a Level 2.1 Support Worker (the most common classification), the 2025-26 base rate is approximately $35.95 per hour. This means a Sunday shift costs $71.90 per hour before superannuation and on-costs.

COMMON SCHADS COMPLIANCE MISTAKES:

1. Not applying Saturday penalty rates to shifts that cross midnight from Friday
2. Calculating overtime based on daily hours when the Award specifies weekly triggers for some clauses
3. Missing broken shift allowances when a client cancellation creates an unpaid gap
4. Not paying sleepover allowances when a worker is on-site overnight even if not called
5. Applying contractor rates to workers who should be classified as employees under the Award
