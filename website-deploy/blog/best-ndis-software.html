Best NDIS Software Australia 2026: 7 Platforms Compared

April 2026 · 14 min read

Choosing NDIS software is one of the biggest decisions an Australian disability provider will make. The right platform saves hours of administration every week, keeps your organisation audit ready, and gives your team the tools to focus on participant outcomes instead of paperwork. The wrong one creates more problems than it solves.

We compared seven NDIS software platforms based on features, pricing structure, ease of use, AI capabilities, and suitability for different provider sizes. This guide covers what each platform does well, where it falls short, and which type of provider it suits best.

1. Titus CRM

Titus CRM is the newest entrant on this list and the most AI forward. Built in Brisbane specifically for Australian disability providers, it takes a fundamentally different approach to NDIS software by embedding artificial intelligence into every major workflow rather than bolting it on as an afterthought.

The standout feature is the AI voice to text progress note system. Support workers tap a microphone icon on their phone, speak naturally about their session, and AI structures the transcript into a compliant NDIS progress note with goals referenced, outcomes identified, and follow up actions extracted. Workers report saving 30 minutes per shift on documentation alone.

Beyond progress notes, the platform includes AI rostering suggestions that factor in SCHADS compliance, staff availability, and participant preferences. The Andy AI chatbot answers staff questions about policies, procedures, and NDIS regulations instantly. A built in AI voice receptionist handles after hours calls and routes enquiries without human intervention. Twilio powered calls and SMS are integrated directly into the CRM, so every communication is logged automatically.

The pricing model is notably different from competitors. Titus CRM charges a flat weekly rate starting at $199 per week with no per user fees. For a provider with 30 staff, that works out significantly cheaper than per user platforms where the same team would cost $270 or more per month. The platform supports seven distinct user roles (Director, Team Leader, Rostering Officer, Admin, Support Worker, Independent Contractor, and Auditor) and delivers everything through a unified app hub combining CRM, Voice, and Receipts in a single progressive web app.

The real time compliance dashboard shows SCHADS award compliance across every roster, flagging issues before they become audit findings. NDIS plan budget tracking is built into the scheduler so coordinators can see remaining funding while building rosters.

Strengths

  • Full AI suite across documentation, rostering, and communications
  • Flat pricing saves money as you grow
  • Built in Twilio calls and SMS
  • SCHADS compliant rostering with real time warnings
  • Seven role types with granular permissions

Limitations

  • Newer platform with smaller market presence
  • PWA rather than native app store listing
  • Fewer third party accounting integrations than ShiftCare
Best For

Providers who want cutting edge AI automation and predictable flat rate pricing. Particularly strong for teams of 15 to 100 staff where per user pricing becomes expensive.

2. ShiftCare

ShiftCare is the largest NDIS specific software platform in Australia and has built its reputation on reliability and breadth of integrations. If your provider already runs MYOB, Xero, KeyPay, or QuickBooks for accounting, ShiftCare is likely the smoothest integration path available.

The platform covers rostering, client management, progress notes, invoicing, and compliance documentation. The accounting integrations are genuinely best in class. Timesheets flow directly into payroll software, invoices sync to your accounting package, and financial reporting pulls data from both systems. For providers where the finance team drives software decisions, this is a major selling point.

ShiftCare also offers a job board feature that lets providers advertise available shifts to a pool of workers, which is useful for organisations that rely on casual or agency staff. The Premium tier includes eMAR medication tracking, which is essential for SIL providers managing medication administration records. AI summaries were added recently on the Premium plan, though the AI capabilities are limited compared to platforms built with AI from the ground up.

The platform holds a 4.6 out of 5 rating on Capterra, reflecting strong user satisfaction. The mobile app is well designed and workers generally find it intuitive after a short onboarding period.

The main drawback is the per user pricing model. Starting at $9 per user per month on the basic plan, costs escalate quickly for larger teams. A 50 person organisation on the Premium tier could be paying $500 or more per month. The platform is also showing its age in some areas, with the interface feeling more functional than modern compared to newer competitors.

Strengths

  • Best accounting integrations (MYOB, Xero, KeyPay, QuickBooks)
  • Established and trusted platform
  • Job board for shift advertising
  • eMAR medication tracking on Premium
  • Strong mobile app

Limitations

  • Per user pricing gets expensive at scale
  • Limited AI capabilities
  • Interface feels dated in some areas
  • Premium features locked behind higher tiers
Best For

Established providers who need deep accounting software integrations and are willing to pay per user for a proven, reliable platform.

3. Brevity Care

Brevity Care has carved out a strong niche serving large SIL (Supported Independent Living) providers and organisations managing complex care arrangements. If your primary service delivery involves 24/7 residential support with multiple participants sharing accommodation, Brevity understands that workflow better than most.

The platform excels at participant management for complex funding models. SIL ratio calculations, shared cost allocation between participants in the same property, and detailed support plan documentation are all handled natively. NDIS claiming tools help providers generate bulk payment requests and reconcile funding against delivered supports.

Brevity also handles incident management and restrictive practices reporting well, which is critical for SIL providers who face more intensive regulatory scrutiny. The compliance documentation tools are thorough, covering everything from behaviour support plans to medication management records.

The downsides are significant for smaller providers. The interface feels dated compared to platforms built in the last few years, with a steeper learning curve for staff who are not tech savvy. Enterprise pricing is not transparent, meaning you need to go through a sales process to get a quote. This typically puts Brevity out of reach for small to mid size providers. The platform also has limited AI features, relying on traditional form based data entry for most documentation tasks.

Strengths

  • Deep SIL and complex care functionality
  • Strong NDIS claiming and funding tools
  • Comprehensive incident and restrictive practices management
  • Handles shared SIL cost allocation

Limitations

  • Dated interface with steep learning curve
  • Enterprise pricing not transparent
  • Limited AI or automation features
  • Overkill for community access or in home support providers
Best For

Large SIL providers managing complex participant funding, shared accommodation costs, and intensive compliance requirements.

4. SupportAbility

SupportAbility is one of the longest running NDIS software platforms in Australia, and its maturity shows in the depth of customisation available. For providers who have specific workflows that do not fit neatly into a standardised template, SupportAbility offers the flexibility to configure almost everything.

The platform is highly customisable across forms, fields, reports, and workflows. If your organisation has a particular way of documenting participant interactions, tracking goal progress, or generating management reports, SupportAbility can likely be configured to match. The reporting engine is particularly strong, allowing providers to build custom reports that pull data from across the system.

SupportAbility also handles NDIS plan management and claiming well, with tools for tracking service bookings against plan budgets and generating bulk payment requests. The platform integrates with several payroll and accounting systems, though not as seamlessly as ShiftCare.

The trade off for all this flexibility is complexity. New users frequently describe a steep learning curve, and the initial setup requires significant configuration time. Many providers engage SupportAbility's implementation team or a consultant to get the system set up properly, which adds to the total cost of ownership. Pricing is quote based, so you will not find a public pricing page. This usually means it is not cheap, but the exact cost depends on your organisation's size and requirements.

The platform has no meaningful AI features as of 2026, relying entirely on manual data entry for documentation and rostering decisions.

Strengths

  • Highly customisable forms, fields, and workflows
  • Strong reporting engine
  • Mature platform with deep NDIS knowledge
  • Good plan management and claiming tools

Limitations

  • Steep learning curve
  • Quote based pricing (not transparent)
  • Requires significant setup and configuration time
  • No AI features
Best For

Mid size providers (20 to 80 staff) who have specific workflow requirements and want deep customisation, and are willing to invest time in setup and training.

5. Lumary (now Salesforce)

Lumary was one of Australia's early NDIS software platforms and was acquired by Salesforce in 2024. The acquisition brought enterprise grade CRM capabilities to the NDIS space, but it also brought enterprise grade complexity and pricing.

Built on the Salesforce platform, Lumary now has access to the full Salesforce ecosystem including Einstein AI, Flow automation builder, and the AppExchange marketplace. For large organisations with dedicated IT teams, this opens up powerful possibilities. Custom dashboards, automated workflows that span multiple departments, and integration with virtually any other enterprise system are all achievable.

The NDIS specific functionality covers participant management, service delivery tracking, plan management, and claiming. The platform handles high volume operations well and is designed for organisations managing hundreds or thousands of participants across multiple service lines.

The reality for most Australian NDIS providers, however, is that Lumary is too complex and too expensive. Enterprise pricing typically runs into thousands of dollars per month, and most implementations require an implementation partner (a Salesforce consulting firm) to configure and deploy the system properly. The total cost of ownership including licenses, implementation, and ongoing support can easily exceed $100,000 in the first year for a mid size provider.

The interface is Salesforce, which means it is powerful but not intuitive for support workers who need to complete documentation quickly on a mobile device. Training requirements are substantial.

Strengths

  • Enterprise grade CRM on Salesforce platform
  • Einstein AI and Flow automation
  • Handles very high volume operations
  • Integrates with the entire Salesforce ecosystem

Limitations

  • Extremely expensive (enterprise pricing)
  • Requires implementation partner for setup
  • Complex interface with steep learning curve
  • Overkill for providers under 100 staff
Best For

Large enterprise providers (100+ staff) with dedicated IT teams and budget for a Salesforce implementation. Not practical for small or mid size providers.

6. Vertex360

Vertex360 positions itself as the affordable entry point for NDIS software, and for new or very small providers operating on tight budgets, it delivers on that promise. Starting at just $5 per user per month, it is the cheapest option on this list by a significant margin.

The platform covers the basics well: rostering, client management, progress notes, incident reporting, and basic compliance documentation. The interface is clean and straightforward, which means less training time for workers who may not be comfortable with technology. For a provider with five to ten staff who just needs to get rosters out, document sessions, and maintain basic compliance records, Vertex360 does the job without unnecessary complexity.

Vertex360 also includes NDIS claiming tools and basic invoicing, so providers can manage their funding submissions without a separate system. The mobile app covers the essentials for field workers.

The limitations become apparent as a provider grows. There are no AI features for documentation or rostering. Advanced compliance tools like SCHADS award rate calculations are limited. Reporting is basic compared to platforms like SupportAbility or ShiftCare. Integrations with accounting software are either limited or nonexistent. For a provider that starts on Vertex360 and grows beyond 20 staff, a platform migration becomes likely within 12 to 18 months.

Strengths

  • Most affordable option (from $5/user/month)
  • Clean, simple interface
  • Low training requirements
  • Covers basic rostering, notes, and compliance

Limitations

  • No AI features
  • Limited reporting and analytics
  • Few third party integrations
  • Likely outgrown as provider scales beyond 20 staff
Best For

Brand new or very small providers (under 15 staff) who need basic NDIS software at the lowest possible cost and can accept limited features.

7. Comm.care

Comm.care takes a simplicity first approach to NDIS software. Where other platforms try to be everything to everyone, Comm.care focuses on doing rostering, progress notes, and basic client management cleanly and without friction. For providers who value ease of use over feature depth, that focus is appealing.

The platform's rostering interface is intuitive and well designed. Creating shifts, assigning workers, and managing availability is straightforward even for coordinators who are not particularly tech savvy. The progress note system is form based and structured, guiding workers through the required fields without overwhelming them with options.

Comm.care has a solid mobile app that workers generally find easy to use. Clock in and clock out functionality, shift details, and progress note entry all work reliably on mobile. The platform also handles basic NDIS invoicing and supports direct connections to the NDIS portal for claiming.

Starting at $6 per user per month, pricing is competitive, though it adds up for larger teams. The per user model means a 40 person team would pay $240 per month, which is comparable to Titus CRM's flat rate but with significantly fewer features.

The gaps are in advanced features. There are no AI capabilities for documentation or rostering. SCHADS compliance checking is basic. There is no built in recruitment pipeline, no LMS, no integrated communications (calls or SMS), and limited customisation options. Comm.care is a solid tool for what it does, but providers needing a comprehensive platform will find it lacking.

Strengths

  • Simple, clean, and easy to learn
  • Good mobile app experience
  • Intuitive rostering interface
  • Competitive pricing for small teams

Limitations

  • No AI features
  • Limited SCHADS compliance tools
  • No recruitment, LMS, or communications features
  • Per user pricing adds up for larger teams
Best For

Providers who want a simple, no fuss platform for rostering and basic documentation and do not need advanced features, AI, or deep integrations.

How to Choose the Right NDIS Software

There is no single best NDIS software platform. The right choice depends on your organisation's size, budget, service types, and priorities. Here are the key decision criteria.

Team Size

  • Under 20 staff: Vertex360 or Comm.care offer affordable entry points with the basics covered. Titus CRM's flat pricing also works well here if you want AI features from day one.
  • 20 to 100 staff: This is where Titus CRM, ShiftCare, and SupportAbility compete most directly. Flat pricing (Titus) becomes increasingly attractive compared to per user models (ShiftCare) as your team grows.
  • 100+ staff: Lumary (Salesforce) or Brevity Care handle enterprise scale operations, though at enterprise prices. Titus CRM's flat rate also scales well at this level.

Budget Structure

Per user pricing sounds cheap at small scale but compounds quickly. A provider paying $9 per user per month for 50 users spends $450 per month ($5,400 per year). A flat rate platform like Titus CRM at $199 per week ($10,348 per year) includes all users plus AI features that would be add ons elsewhere. Run the maths for your specific team size before committing.

AI Needs

If AI powered documentation, rostering suggestions, and automated communications are important to your strategy, Titus CRM is the only platform with a full AI suite built in. ShiftCare has limited AI on its Premium tier. Lumary has access to Salesforce Einstein. The remaining four platforms have no meaningful AI features.

Accounting Integration Needs

If seamless integration with MYOB, Xero, KeyPay, or QuickBooks is your top priority, ShiftCare is the strongest option. No other platform matches its depth of accounting software connections.

Complexity of Care

For SIL providers managing 24/7 residential care with complex funding, shared accommodation costs, and intensive compliance requirements, Brevity Care has the deepest specialisation. For general NDIS services including community access, in home support, and group programs, Titus CRM and ShiftCare both handle these workflows well.

The NDIS software market is moving fast. Platforms that relied solely on form based data entry two years ago are now falling behind those that use AI to reduce admin burden and improve compliance. When evaluating your options, consider not just what you need today but where the sector is heading.
